WEEK 01 · INVESTING FUNDAMENTALS

The power of compound growth.

Why time can be one of an investor's greatest advantages.

15–20 MIN READ

01 · LEARN

Imagine this...

You invest €10,000. After one year, your investment grows by 7%. You now have €10,700.

In the second year, you earn 7% on €10,700, not just on your original €10,000.

Your returns are now generating their own returns. This is called compound growth.

Your money earns returns, and those returns can earn returns too.

Why does time matter?

At the beginning, growth can seem slow. But as your investment becomes larger, the same percentage return generates more money.

This is why starting earlier can make a significant difference over long periods.

02 · UNDERSTAND

Sara and Tom

Sara starts saving at 25. She invests €200 a month for ten years, then stops at 35 and never adds another euro.

Tom waits until he is 35. Then he invests the same €200 a month for thirty years, right up to 65. Tom puts in three times as much money as Sara.

Both earn 7% a year. Who has more at 65?

SARA · INVESTS FROM 25 TO 35 €260,418

She put in €24,000.

TOM · INVESTS FROM 35 TO 65 €233,891

He put in €72,000.

Sara ends up with more, even though she stopped adding money thirty years earlier. Her early euros simply had more time to compound.

What should investors remember?

  • Compounding can make long-term investing powerful.
  • Investment returns are not guaranteed.
  • Fees and taxes reduce what you keep.
  • Inflation reduces the purchasing power of money.
  • Losses can also have a compounding effect.
