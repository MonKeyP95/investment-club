# Week 1 answer key: Compound growth

**Admin only. Do not post this file in the group.** Share the answers after people have had a chance to reply and discuss.

1. **b) €1,210.** After year one you have €1,100. In year two you earn 10% on €1,100, not on €1,000, so you gain €110 instead of €100. That extra €10 is compound growth.

2. **c) Sara.** She ends with about €260,000 after putting in €24,000. Tom ends with about €234,000 after putting in €72,000. Sara's money had thirty extra years to compound.

3. **a) Both fees and inflation.** Fees take a slice of your return every year, and that missing slice can no longer compound. Inflation means the money you end up with buys less.
